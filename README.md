# Model-agnostic variable importance for survival outcomes: a simulation study under additive, multiplicative and mixed hazards

This simulation study therefore aims to evaluate the performance, robustness 
and inferential validity of the intrinsic importance measure of variables 
in survival analysis under various data-generating mechanisms. 

It has four objectives:

1. To evaluate the method in an additive risk framework
2. To investigate its behaviour when additive and multiplicative effects coexist
3. To examine its sensitivity to the rarity of events and the heterogeneity of their effects
4. To assess the influence of correlations between covariates across the different data-generating mechanisms

Two methods for estimating conditional survival functions are compared: 
***global survival stacking*** and the approach involving the combination of 
survival functions, referred to here as ***survivalSL***.

These different configurations should enable us to better specify the 
conditions under which the non-parametric variable importance method 
provides a reliable estimate of the predictive contribution of the 
variables and correctly calibrated statistical inference in survival analyses.
